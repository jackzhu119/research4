# Cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript “Terahertz fingerprints and machine learning for complementary discrimination of medicinal materials” for publication in Applied Spectroscopy.

Routine medicinal-material identification relies on established morphological, microscopic and chemical tests. This manuscript develops terahertz fingerprint analysis as a complementary research approach by connecting recorded optical representations, machine-learning classification and controlled ablations. Its contribution is an evidence-based workflow for choosing the spectral information and learning updates that support discrimination, providing a methodological reference for subsequent studies of additional medicinal materials.

The collection comprises 1,672 specimens representing Suanzaoren, Zaoren, Huangqi and Tianma. A common absorption classifier correctly classified 174/174 specimens in the seed-material comparison and 331/339 across the four materials. Selected absorption features achieved 93.44% mean accuracy in repeated internal nested validation of balanced five-source Suanzaoren data.

At a fixed budget of 62 predictors, a joint SNV absorption–refractive-index candidate pool achieved 87.11%, compared with 81.00% for SNV absorption selection. All 930 retained feature occurrences belonged to absorption, identifying how candidate-pool integration can change the selected information while ending in single-modality prediction. Raw-absorption and complete internally selected controls specify the comparison scope. Support-label ablations further locate the additional-label gain in valid supervision and decision-function updating, distinguishing this route from changes in preprocessing, loss mass and prediction scores. Two qualified protocatechuic-acid calculations provide a separate molecular frequency-scale reference.

The article connects a medicinal-material application to interpretable spectroscopic and learning choices. Its 28 references establish the analytical motivation and relevant prior work; complete corrected positive and negative comparisons, specimen roles and source receipts accompany the manuscript. The findings provide testable directions for extending terahertz-assisted screening to further materials.
